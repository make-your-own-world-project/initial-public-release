through public documentation explaining its motivations, architecture, experiments,
measured observations, failures, and open questions so others can inspect or reuse
what is useful. It is not a campaign against any person, employer, institution,
platform, industry, government, community, religion, political affiliation, or
worldview.

## Privacy, sovereignty, and do no harm

The public edition is designed to protect the author's sovereignty and the equal
sovereignty of everyone else. A private corpus may preserve interactions, observations,
and surrounding events as part of the owner's own history. Preservation creates no
authority to profile, infer from, extract value from, publish, monetize, predict, or
track the other person. An observer does not acquire another person's life by seeing
it, recording it, or encountering it in public.

Public visibility is not consent. Presence on a road, sidewalk, camera feed, recording,
or other observable setting does not authorize aggregation or secondary use. A stated
security or public-safety purpose does not authorize personal agendas, blackmail,
manipulation, retaliation, or unrelated surveillance. Names, relationship labels,
quotations, movements, private circumstances, attributed motives, and re-identifying
combinations remain excluded without explicit, informed, purpose-bounded authority from
the affected person.

No disclaimer grants permission to use another person’s experience, waives an
affected person’s rights, or excuses foreseeable harm. If a passage is believed to
identify or misrepresent a person, disclose protected information, or exceed a
source’s permission, distribution of that passage should pause for review.

Legal allowance is not treated as proof of legitimate authority. Where law, policy,
contract, or technical access permits collection without meaningful consent, this
release applies the stricter sovereignty boundary. The observed life remains under the
authority of the person living it.

Sharing a bounded payload with an external service is an authorized disclosure only
within the permission, privacy, retention, and contractual terms that actually apply.
The architecture does not assume that a recipient forgets the payload or gains no
value from it. Its narrower claim is that omitted corpus-level information cannot be
reconstructed from a derivative that does not contain it. Provider practices remain
a separate review boundary. The public architectural claim is that useful external
work can occur without transferring the complete human record for extraction into
institutional value.

prove the absence of every defect or risk.
