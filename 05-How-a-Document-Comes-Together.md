# How a Document Comes Together

![A bounded payload moving through specialized planning, realization, verification, and promotion stations](assets/artifact-assembly-line.png)

## Context payloads with typed handoffs

Document generation is treated as an assembly line for a typed context payload. Each
specialist contributes a bounded part of the finished product. A missing field is not
automatically assigned to the adjacent sender. Every field has a semantic owner,
possible contributors, transformer, carrier, consumer, and validator.

The handoff records expected and observed work so the manager can distinguish:

- missing source evidence;
- unavailable capability;
- transformation failure;
- transport loss;
- consumer misuse;
- incompatible contract;
- validation failure.

## Product contract

Before selection, a free-form request becomes a typed contract describing:

- evidence universe and requested aspect;
- receiver and purpose;
- desired reader outcome;
- product or genre;
- language, field, tenor, and channel;
- chronological or relationship perspective;
- length and structural budget;
- evidence, inference, invention, and citation policy;
- assumed knowledge and vocabulary;
- delivery medium and export format.

Audience is a receiver state, not a demographic adjective pasted into a prompt. The
line must determine what can be assumed, what needs introduction, which vocabulary is
decodable, and why the selected material matters to that receiver.

## Reverse expansion

The line walks backward from the request through chronology, typed relationships,
supersession, and open attachment points. Later material can identify earlier
evidence needed to understand it. No final title, thesis, or section plan exists yet,
and no branch is pruned before the provisional structure is visible.

## Whole-tree collapse

Collapse partitions the selected evidence against the jobs required by the product.
It is not topic clustering, nearest-neighbor section filling, or a single-pass
summary.

The process is intentionally lossy but preserves graph shape. Branch size represents
processing cost, not automatic importance. A large secondary branch cannot consume
the product merely because the corpus contains many words about it. Failures,
contradictions, rejected approaches, and unresolved questions survive when they
explain the resulting path.

Interlaced refinement works toward the final shape in repeated passes. Early passes
establish broad balance and ownership. Later passes add detail where it improves the
declared product without breaking section budgets, graph ownership, or provenance.

## Compact working model

The editor receives a portable, product-specific working model rather than the whole
corpus. It contains:

- selected semantic units and exact sources;
- relation and temporal edges;
- uncertainty, conflict, correction, and open-question state;
- product and receiver requirements;
- section and rhetorical jobs;
- ownership and evidence bindings;
- context-only neighborhood used for disambiguation;
- expansion handles for bounded retrieval when something essential is missing.

Context-only material may explain a source unit but cannot silently become prose.

The same rule governs an external handoff. The product line may export a deliberately
bounded payload containing supported material, product requirements, and permitted
context. An outside realizer can contribute presentation quality without receiving
unrestricted corpus access. The payload is a job package, not a transfer of the
maintained knowledge system.

## Editorial realization

The editor's job is delivery, not source-world invention. It performs content
ordering, first-mention expansion, sentence realization, transitions, typography,
morphology, paragraph formation, and product-level continuity. It can request missing
context through the handoff; it cannot undo a prior contribution because it does not
understand it.

A deterministic grounded rendering supplies a safe editorial floor. Optional
generated candidates must beat that floor and pass grounding, product, readability,
Weave, and deslop checks. A fluent candidate receives no authority merely because it
was produced by a capable model.

## Ledger and query-plan analysis

Every run records what each cog received, produced, omitted, cost, and added. The
manager can compare value against latency, memory, accelerator time, retries, and
failure rate. This is analogous to an execution-plan analysis for a context assembly
line: expensive stages must justify their contribution, and underused capabilities
are management problems rather than evidence that the specialist is useless.
