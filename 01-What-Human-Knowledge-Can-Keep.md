# What Human Knowledge Can Keep

![Fragments of human context losing their relationships in ordinary storage](assets/ordinary-storage-loses-context.png)

This is not a national, governmental, professional, or ideological program. It is one
individual's answer to a global human problem, built from knowledge created across
languages, cultures, disciplines, and generations. Its operation is local and
reproducible; its sovereignty principles apply to every person at once.

## The information that disappears

Digital systems are good at preserving objects and poor at preserving the lived path
between them. A folder may contain notes, questions, images, tasks, conversations,
experiments, and later corrections. Each item can be searchable while the reason the
collection exists remains inaccessible.

The missing information is not merely a topic label or sentiment score. It includes:

- what prompted attention;
- what was being attempted or avoided;
- which alternatives were considered;
- what failed and why that failure mattered;
- what changed in response to new evidence;
- what remained unknown;
- which external circumstances constrained the action;
- how an old observation became relevant again;
- why the resulting thread might matter to another person.

A factual answer without that structure can be technically correct while offering
limited practical value. The system therefore treats the reconstruction of concern,
reasoning, and change as the primary product. Storage, search, embeddings, models,
and documents are supporting machinery.

## Deposits and withdrawals

An artifact entering the preserved corpus is a **deposit**. A deposit may be a note,
document, image, task, recording, message, recollection, plan, question, or unresolved
datapoint. Its existence is sufficient to give it standing. It does not need to
contain a valid argument, identified motive, solved problem, or presently useful fact.

A retrieved answer, analysis, guide, article, or other generated product is a
**withdrawal**. A withdrawal selects and compresses because no bounded product can
contain the whole source world. That loss is acceptable when it is declared,
product-specific, and reversible through provenance. It is not acceptable when the
withdrawal rewrites the deposit, hides uncertainty, or becomes the only surviving
representation.

## Constructive condensation

Large centralized representations extract human knowledge and convert it into reusable
institutional capability by discarding distinctions that do not help their aggregate
objective. Individual provenance, rare alternatives, changing interpretations, and
the reason one person cared can be destroyed even when the resulting model is capable
and fluent. The institution retains the generalized value while the human record loses
what made the contribution meaningful. Adding more context later cannot recover a
distinction that was never retained.

The system keeps the authorized corpus fixed and treats interpretations,
indexes, compact contexts, and generated products as replaceable overlays. New
evidence can change how earlier material is understood without requiring the earlier
material to have predicted that change. Condensation becomes constructive because
the system can generate useful bounded views while retaining the basis for a
different view later.

## The Tower of Babel inversion

The design uses the Tower of Babel as an architectural metaphor for a design risk:
many local meanings are absorbed into one common representation, fluency is mistaken
for mutual understanding, and the paths back to individual reasons disappear.

The inverse architecture keeps local evidence owned and independently addressable,
allows plural representations, preserves unknowns, and treats translation as a
versioned withdrawal for a particular receiver. No one model is required to
understand the whole system, and no common output language becomes the source of
truth.

## What success looks like

For a representative evidence slice, the system should reconstruct enough of the
concern and reasoning lifecycle that a reader can understand:

1. what happened or was considered;
2. why attention was invested, when evidence establishes it;
3. what questions, alternatives, failures, and evidence shaped the path;
4. what changed or remained unresolved;
5. why the reconstructed thread could matter to the intended reader.

Unknown remains an acceptable answer. Invented motive is not.

Success also means preventing that destructive exchange while retaining useful outside
capability. A bounded payload or withdrawal can be shared for one declared operation
while the human record, provenance, reconstruction machinery, personal meaning, and
authority to create future withdrawals remain under the owner's control. Without the
extraction and destruction of that knowledge for institutional gain, the centralized
top-down business model loses its foundation.
