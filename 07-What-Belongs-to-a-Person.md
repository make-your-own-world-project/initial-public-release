# What Belongs to a Person

## Declaration

A person's experience, identity, attention, history, relationships, expression, and
capacity to decide what they mean are not raw material acquired by another person,
institution, platform, employer, model, or market merely because it offers
convenience, money, access, influence, entertainment, or participation.

Widespread surrender does not establish rightful ownership. A click, boilerplate
term, payment, or continued use is not sufficient evidence that a person knowingly
transferred something they would otherwise retain.

Valid authorization must be informed, specific, comprehensible, freely given,
bounded to a declared purpose and recipient, evidenced, and revocable where
revocation remains possible. Deception, coercion, manipulation, dark patterns,
materially incomplete disclosure, obscured consequences, or silent purpose expansion
cannot create valid authorization in this architecture. Availability is not
permission.

## Reciprocal sovereignty

The rule applies equally to everyone represented in the corpus. Preserving one
person's experience cannot depend on harvesting another person's identity, words,
traits, motives, private facts, or inner life. The owner can authorize disclosure of
the owner's own experience. That authority does not transfer another person's
sovereignty.

The corpus may preserve personal interactions, observations of people, and events in
the owner's surroundings as part of the owner's own historical record. Preservation
does not create permission to use those records against or about the other person. It
does not authorize profiling, inference, extraction, publication, monetization,
behavioral prediction, movement tracking, or derivative value, even when doing so would
directly benefit the observer or the system. That authority belongs to the person whose
life would be used.

Observation is not ownership. Visibility is not consent. A person does not surrender
sovereignty by entering a public road, walking through a camera's field of view, speaking
within earshot, appearing in an interaction, or being noticed. Recording a neighbor's
departures and returns from a public street does not create authority to publish,
aggregate, interpret, or exploit that movement history. A security or public-safety
purpose does not silently expand into personal access, blackmail, manipulation,
retaliation, curiosity, or any other secondary agenda.

Legal permission and legitimate authority are separate questions in this architecture.
A law, policy, contract, camera placement, or technical capability may permit collection
without establishing informed human consent. The system therefore applies the stricter
boundary: another person's observed life remains theirs. If explicit, informed,
purpose-bounded authority is absent, the observation remains private and unavailable
for extraction or publication. The long pattern of treating human visibility as
available raw material stops at this boundary.

## Observation is not endorsement

An observation event records that a perspective, reaction, question, metaphor, or
report occurred in project history. The proposition inside it may be supported,
disputed, unknown, superseded, metaphorical, or outside the verifier's scope.

Preserving the event does not prove the proposition or turn it into a current
personal belief. Publication may accurately present a source-bound project view while
keeping its epistemic role and historical state. It must not launder a specific view
into anonymous consensus or attribute it to a person, employer, collaborator,
institution, or community without authority.

## Public derivation rules

Public material must:

- preserve source and transformation lineage;
- omit direct identifiers, relationship labels, private communications, and
  re-identifying combinations;
- remove private work cadence while retaining an abstract sequence when needed;
- distinguish observation, factual claim, interpretation, metaphor, value judgment,
  question, forecast, plan, failure, correction, quotation, and unknown;
- retain lawful controversial positions with their actual authority and role rather
  than erasing them for convenience;
- verify rights and attribution for every redistributed contribution;
- bind review and distribution to exact artifact bytes;
- fail closed when a hard sovereignty, safety, rights, or security boundary remains
  unresolved.

## Review does not manufacture clearance

Automated checks detect and route possible issues. They do not become legal counsel,
an employer, a rights holder, or the affected person. Absence of a detector match
means only that the detector found no match.

Publication review separates privacy and third-party sovereignty, employment and
conflict, copyright and licensing, legal and defamation, security, financial claims,
accessibility, and public interpretation. A reviewer owns decisions only within that
bounded responsibility.

## Reciprocal protection

Do no harm is a reciprocal hard gate. A system built to prevent others from
appropriating or damaging one person's experience cannot achieve that protection by
doing the same thing to someone else. It must not expose, exploit, stigmatize,
diagnose, blame, endanger, or appropriate another person's experience for a useful
story.

Intent is not sufficient. Foreseeable harm from identification, re-identification,
private disclosure, unwanted attribution, missing context, false authority, or use
beyond permission remains a blocker even when nobody meant harm. The acceptable
responses are to preserve the idea at a safer and genuinely generic level, obtain the
affected authority, or keep the material private.

## Human knowledge remains intact and under owner control

![A complete human record remaining local while only a bounded derivative reaches optional external machinery](assets/human-knowledge-sovereignty-inversion.png)

The central sovereignty problem is not that people must rent general human knowledge.
It is that centralized systems extract human contributions, discard the provenance,
continuity, relationships, and personal meaning that do not serve an aggregate model,
and retain the resulting institutional value. What mattered to the person is destroyed
while what scales for the institution survives. Without that extraction and destructive
reduction, the centralized top-down business model loses its foundation.

This architecture reverses that exchange. Personal knowledge and preserved experience
remain intact and usable under the owner's control. Local operation makes continuity a
property of the installation rather than a promise from the system receiving the
contribution. The operating result returns value taken from humanity to human control:
the record, meaning, provenance, and capacity to create future value remain with the
people whose knowledge made that value possible.

The same principle applies to humanity's shared record. A system should not erase the
conditions, people, purposes, and changing meanings behind contributed work to produce
a more marketable centralized capability. Useful derivation does not confer ownership
over the experience from which it was derived. The purpose here is to preserve the
human record and increase the individual's practical authority over it, not to
reproduce the extraction pattern at a smaller scale.

The same principle informs reciprocity toward the public commons. People and
institutions made literature, research, software, standards, measurements, and other
work inspectable under many different permissions. Public availability is not a
blanket transfer of ownership, endorsement, or consent to enclose that collective
contribution while concentrating its derived value inside one institution. Exact
rights still govern each source; the architectural principle is that permitted
knowledge should remain reproducible, attributable, and connected to the people,
conditions, and meanings that made it valuable.

Selective disclosure makes that principle practical. An owner may authorize a bounded
payload for a specific external operation while retaining the corpus, provenance,
reconstruction machinery, and continuing authority from which it was derived. The
recipient gains the material necessary for the declared contribution, not the human
record whose destruction and absorption would create durable institutional value. It
receives neither ownership of the preserved asset nor authority over later use.
Provider retention practices and contractual terms remain separate permissions that
must be evaluated before any external handoff.

## Project purpose

The work is an engineering, preservation, and communication effort. It is not an effort
to damage, disable, overthrow, sabotage, threaten, or incite action against people,
systems, organizations, institutions, communities, beliefs, or worldviews. Strong
systemic criticism can remain when accurately typed as critique, observation, or
metaphor. It does not become an accusation or operational instruction through
publication.

The public project is an act of reciprocity toward the research, public-domain work,
technical frameworks, cultural material, institutions, and machine systems that made
the engineering possible. Gratitude does not replace item-level permission, and
criticism does not imply hostility.

Attribution is how that reciprocity becomes visible. The public record uses outside work
because people and institutions chose to share, license, archive, translate, or
dedicate it for others. Each material contribution should identify who shared it,
where it came from, what permission made the use possible, what part informed this
project, and what remains the contributor's work. Adoption, measurement, evaluation,
influence, and rejected experiments are different relationships and are labelled as
such. Attribution never implies that a contributor endorses this project.
